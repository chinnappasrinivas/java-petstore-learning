1. How do you approach Agile coaching in a product-driven organization?

Answer:
“I start by aligning Agile practices with business outcomes rather than enforcing frameworks. In product-driven environments, success is measured by user value, speed, and adaptability.

In my recent role, I worked closely with Product Owners to ensure backlog prioritization reflected business goals. I assessed team maturity, identified bottlenecks, and tailored coaching at three levels—team, program, and leadership.

For example, by improving backlog clarity and strengthening sprint goals, we achieved better alignment between delivery and business expectations while maintaining a steady delivery flow.


2. How have you improved delivery predictability in your teams?

Answer:
“In one of my programs, we faced frequent sprint spillovers due to unmanaged cross-team dependencies.

I introduced structured Scrum-of-Scrums, improved dependency tracking in Jira, and ensured alignment during PI Planning. I also coached teams on realistic sprint commitments based on historical velocity.

As a result, we saw a significant improvement in sprint predictability, reduced carryover work, and more stable delivery patterns across teams.”


3. How do you handle resistance to Agile practices?

Answer:
“I see resistance as a signal, not a problem. My approach is to understand the root cause—whether it’s lack of clarity, previous bad experiences, or perceived overhead.

For example, when stakeholders felt ceremonies were too time-consuming, I streamlined them to focus on outcomes rather than rituals. Once they saw faster decision-making and better visibility, adoption improved naturally.

I focus on demonstrating value rather than enforcing change.”


4. Can you describe your experience with scaling Agile?

Answer:
“I have extensive experience working in SAFe environments with Agile Release Trains.

I supported Release Train Engineers in planning and executing PI Planning, System Demos, and Inspect & Adapt sessions. I also facilitated Scrum-of-Scrums to manage dependencies across distributed teams.

My key contribution was ensuring alignment between teams and program-level goals, which helped improve coordination, reduce bottlenecks, and maintain a continuous delivery flow.


5. How do you measure team performance without relying on vanity metrics?

Answer:
“I focus on meaningful metrics that reflect value delivery and flow.

These include predictability, cycle time, throughput, and quality indicators such as defect leakage and adherence to Definition of Done. I also consider team health and collaboration.

Using Jira dashboards, I ensured transparency and used metrics as a tool for improvement—not evaluation—helping teams continuously refine their processes.”

6. How do you collaborate with Product Owners and Product Managers?

Answer:
“I work closely with Product Owners to ensure the backlog is both valuable and actionable.

I coach them on writing clear user stories, prioritizing based on business value, and maintaining a well-refined backlog. I also facilitate discussions between business and technical teams to ensure shared understanding.

This collaboration helps teams deliver the right features at the right time while minimizing rework.


7. Tell me about a challenging cross-team dependency you resolved.

Answer:
“In a distributed setup, multiple teams were dependent on a shared component, causing frequent delays.

I introduced visibility through dependency boards and facilitated alignment sessions during Scrum-of-Scrums. I also worked with Product Owners to prioritize dependency-related work earlier in the sprint.

This proactive approach reduced blockers significantly and improved coordination across teams.”

8. How do you improve Agile maturity within teams?

Answer:
“I follow a structured approach—assess, coach, enable, and step back.

I start by evaluating current practices and identifying gaps. Then I introduce targeted improvements, such as better retrospectives or clearer sprint goals. I also mentor Scrum Masters and encourage teams to take ownership.

Over time, teams become more self-organizing, and my role shifts from directing to supporting.”


9. What would be your plan for the first 90 days in this role?

Answer:
“In the first 30 days, I would focus on understanding the organization—teams, products, workflows, and challenges.

From 30 to 60 days, I would work on stabilizing delivery by improving ceremonies, addressing bottlenecks, and enhancing transparency.

From 60 to 90 days, I would introduce measurable improvements, such as optimizing flow, reducing dependencies, and aligning delivery more closely with business outcomes.”
